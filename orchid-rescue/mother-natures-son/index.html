Mother Nature’s Son

Mother Nature’s Son was supposed to be the next project.

He arrived as a young Better-Gro Cattleya in a mesh sleeve, standing tall enough to be optimistic and planted loosely enough to fall over whenever he felt like it. Compared with the Phalaenopsis orchids, he wanted more light, more drying time, and considerably less fussing. The first assignment was simple: keep him upright and learn to leave him alone.

That became difficult in March, when the roots started looking worse even as a new green shoot appeared at the base. By April, the old root system had deteriorated enough to force the issue. I removed the dead roots, repotted him in fresh bark, charcoal, and perlite, and waited to find out whether the new growth had enough stored energy to continue.

It did, but not dramatically.

The new lead grew a little at a time. The leaves stayed firm. After the move from Florida to Texas, Mother Nature’s Son came indoors under grow lights because the new patio was regularly over 100 degrees. He accepted that change too and kept building the same lead.

On July 12, it was clearly becoming the next pseudobulb. A week later it was taller. On July 26, a bright white root with a green tip appeared from its base.

That root was the whole story. The new growth was no longer surviving on what the older pseudobulbs had saved for it. It was building a root system of its own.

Mother Nature’s Son never staged a dramatic comeback. He spent six months quietly becoming a plant that no longer needed one.
